Pricing

Free, and you bring the model

AryaAI itself costs nothing and is MIT licensed. You supply a model API key from a provider you already pay, or one you choose, and AryaAI uses it. There is no account, no seat, and nothing to cancel.

Preview release — read before you install

  • AryaAI is in beta, and none of its automation tools have been field tested or proven. Free means free to evaluate, not ready for production work.
  • Installing an unsigned build to write PLC programs or HMI screens is done at your own risk. The installer is not code signed.
  • While online with a controller, AryaAI is read only. It will never be given the ability to download, force, toggle, write, or change data on a live PLC. Every edit happens in offline files.
  • The first milestone is Version 1.0. A paid tier is not one of them — see below.

What the free plan is

The whole application, without a feature gate

Nothing in AryaAI is held back for a paid tier. The engineering servers, the drawings, the plugins, the workflows and the updates are all in the download.

Included, and staying included

  • Every engineering server, including the ones that reach a running IDE
  • DXF drawings, workspaces, plans, skills and workflows
  • The Plugin Market, and plugins you install yourself
  • Updates, including the ones that keep chats and settings
  • Your key stays in your own credential store, and your traffic goes straight to your provider

Not included, and why

  • Model inference — you pay your provider, not us
  • A hosted option for networks that block outbound API calls
  • A support contract, an SLA, or a named account manager
  • Anything a code-signing certificate would fix — the installer is still unsigned

The last one is a genuine inconvenience, not a sales tactic. It is the first thing on the list to fix.

Honest arithmetic

What bringing your own key actually costs

Your spend is set by your provider and by how much you use the agent, not by a plan you picked in advance. That is the point of it.

A concrete example, at published rates Models are billed per million tokens, and input is far cheaper than output. DeepSeek publishes these rates for its own fast model: $0.15 per million input tokens and $0.60 per million output tokens, with a peak rate of $0.30 and $1.20, and off-peak hours at half price. A day spent reading exports, drawings and program files is measured in single-digit millions of tokens, so the arithmetic is dollars rather than tens of dollars. Check your own provider's current prices — these change, and the discount window is theirs to set.

Who holds the key
You do, on your own machine, in your own credential store. AryaAI talks to the provider you configured; nothing is proxied through a server of ours, because there is no server of ours in the path.
What we add on top
Nothing. There is no platform fee, no per-token markup, and no usage we meter. Your provider bills you directly and we are not part of that transaction.
What happens without a key
AryaAI opens and the engineering tools that read files work. The agent needs a model, so anything conversational needs a key before it will run.

Why this page is short

We are not going to invent prices for plans that do not exist

A hosted option is planned. It is not built, not priced, and not priced because pricing it honestly needs something we do not have yet: what a real engineering workload costs.

What is settled
AryaAI is free, MIT licensed, and uses a key you provide. That will not change under anyone who already downloaded it.
What is not settled
Whether a hosted tier exists, what it includes, what it costs, and whether a team arrangement is offered at all. None of that has a date attached.
What we will not do
Put a price on this page to look established, then quietly change it, or gate a feature that the free plan already ships. Both are worse than a short page.
If you want the hosted option
Say so in the issue tracker and say what you would need it for — a locked-down plant network is a different problem from a preference, and the answer changes what gets built.
